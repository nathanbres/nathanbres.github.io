How It Works

Every WAR has a dollar value on the open market. At ~$8M/WAR in 2025, a player producing 4 WAR is theoretically worth $32M. If they earn less, the team captures surplus. This works for both batters (PA-filtered) and pitchers (IP-filtered).

Data Sources

Batting + pitching WAR from Baseball Reference. Salaries from Spotrac. Merged on player name. Batters: 200+ PA. Pitchers: 50+ IP. Both full regular seasons, 2025 and 2026.

Market Rate

Set at $8M / WAR based on 2025 free agent contract benchmarks. Represents what teams pay per win on the open market.

Surplus Value

(WAR × $8M) − Salary. Positive = team bargain. Negative = overpaid vs production. Batters use OPS as context; pitchers use RA9.

Context Matters

Contract status dramatically affects interpretation. Pre-arb surplus is structural. The most valuable analysis targets arb and free agent under/overpays.

⚠ Limitation: Contract Status

Status is approximated from salary only: <$800K = Pre-Arb in 2025 (<$1M in 2026, after the minimum salary rose), up to $7M = Arb, above = FA. Players like Perdomo or Witt Jr. signed early extensions above the minimum, so they may appear as Arb when technically still pre-arb. Exact MLB service time data would be required for full accuracy.

Contract Status Explained

MLB's pay structure creates systematic surplus for young players. Understanding the contract category is key to reading the data correctly.

Pre-Arbitration (Years 1–3)

Salary near the league minimum (~$720K). Surplus is massive by design. Young stars like Perdomo or Witt Jr. appear "undervalued" structurally, not because the market misses them.

Salary Arbitration (Years 4–6)

Salary rises to 20–80% of market value based on performance comparables. Surplus here is more meaningful. Teams that signed these players cheaply made smart calls.

Free Agent

Full market value. Surplus should be near zero in theory. Players showing large positive surplus here are genuinely underpaid. Large negative = aging curve / injury risk materialized.

Model Methodology Market rate derivation, data pipeline, and 2026 season notes ▼

The surplus value framework converts WAR into dollars using an open-market rate, then compares that to actual salaries to identify mispriced contracts. Two full regular seasons are available, 2025 and 2026, switchable at the top of the page.

The Surplus Value Formula

Surplus Value ($M) = (WAR × $8M) − Actual Salary

Positive = underpaid relative to production. Negative = overpaid. The $8M/WAR rate is the estimated free-agent market price per win in 2025.

Why $8M per WAR?

$MFree-agent benchmarks

Analysis of 2023–2025 free-agent signings shows the market has paid approximately $7.5M–$9M per projected WAR. $8M is the midpoint estimate.

!FA rate only

Pre-arb and arbitration players are structurally underpaid versus this rate. Their surplus reflects MLB labor rules (service time, pre-arbitration salary caps), not individual market mispricing.

This model applies a single $8M/WAR rate uniformly across all positions. In practice, catchers, shortstops, and center fielders command positional premiums. The framework intentionally ignores positional context to keep surplus value calculations clean and comparable.

Key Findings

Patterns that emerge across batters and pitchers combined.

Best Surplus: Pre-Arb Stars, Both Years

In 2025, Paul Skenes (+$63.1M) and Cal Raleigh (+$56.6M) led. In 2026, Pete Crow-Armstrong tops all players at +$77.5M (9.8 WAR for $894K), ahead of pitcher Cristopher Sánchez (+$61.8M, 8.1 WAR for $3M).

Worst Contracts of 2026

Kyle Tucker is the most overpaid player at −$54.4M (0.7 WAR on a $60M average annual value), followed by Zac Gallen (−$35.6M), Luis Castillo (−$34.8M) and Jose Altuve (−$30.0M, overpaid in both seasons).

Surplus Only Partly Persists

For the 436 players in both seasons, surplus correlates at 0.39 from 2025 to 2026. The 2025 top 20 averaged +$44.6M, then +$23.6M in 2026. Of 32 players overpaid by $10M+ in 2025, half were still overpaid in 2026.

The Ohtani & Pre-Arb Caveats

Ohtani combines batting and pitching for 6.4 WAR in 2026, still −$18.8M against $70M. Pre-arb players dominate the top in both years: in 2026 they average +$11.2M of surplus, arbitration players +$7.0M, free agents −$2.1M. That gap is MLB labor rules, not market inefficiency.

So What?
The Market Rewards Youth, and Free Agent Bargains Rarely Last

Two full seasons confirm the structural pattern: the largest surplus values sit with pre-arbitration and early arbitration players, the deepest negative values with free agents on long-term deals. That is MLB labor rules by design. The actionable question is what happens within the free agent class, and the second season gives a clear answer: bargains fade fast.

Of the best free agent contracts of 2025, only Bobby Witt Jr. (+$49.8M, then +$40.6M) and Yoshinobu Yamamoto (+$29.2M, then +$35.2M) held their value. Tarik Skubal fell from +$41M to +$4.8M, Nico Hoerner from +$38.1M to +$4.8M, and Aaron Judge from +$37.6M to −$24.8M after playing only 285 PA. Among free agents, surplus correlates at just 0.30 from one season to the next.

Overpays are stickier than bargains: half of the players overpaid by $10M or more in 2025 were still overpaid in 2026, with Jose Altuve (−$25.2M, then −$30.0M) and Sean Manaea (−$27.4M, then −$26.6M) the clearest repeats. And the most expensive 2026 signing, Kyle Tucker (4 years, $240M), produced 0.7 WAR in year one. The implication for front offices: a single strong season is weak evidence for a long contract, and extension timing matters as much as extension value.

Glossary

Contract Value Analysis
Pos
Primary position: the one played most often that season, per Baseball Reference (C, 1B, 2B, 3B, SS, LF, CF, RF, DH). Pitchers are SP (starter, at least half of appearances as a start) or RP (reliever). Shohei Ohtani is listed DH/SP.
WAR
Wins Above Replacement. Estimates how many wins a player adds over a replacement-level player. 2 = average, 5+ = All-Star, 8+ = MVP-caliber.
Surplus Value
Gap between market value (WAR x $8M) and actual salary. Positive = underpaid relative to production, negative = overpaid.
Market Rate ($8M/WAR)
Estimated cost of one win on the free-agent market (~$8M in 2025). Converts WAR into an expected salary benchmark.
Pre-Arb
Pre-Arbitration. Players in their first ~3 years of MLB service, earning near the league minimum (~$760K-$800K in 2025).
Arbitration
Players with 3-6 years of service who negotiate salaries via a formal process. Typical range: $1M-$15M.
Free Agent
Players with 6+ years of MLB service who can sign with any team. Command the highest market salaries.
2026 WAR
Full 2026 regular season WAR from Baseball Reference. For Shohei Ohtani, batting and pitching WAR are combined.
IP
Innings Pitched. One inning = three outs recorded. Measures a pitcher's total workload.
2TM / 3TM
Two or three teams. Indicates a player was traded mid-season; stats are combined across all teams played for.
