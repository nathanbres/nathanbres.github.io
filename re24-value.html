How It Works

This analysis follows the run expectancy method taught in the University of Michigan Sports Performance Analytics program. Instead of counting outcomes like RBI, it values every plate appearance by how much it changed the team's chances of scoring, given the situation it happened in.

1. The Situations

A half-inning is always in one of 24 states: 8 combinations of runners on base times 0, 1 or 2 outs.

2. The Matrix

For each state, the average number of runs scored until the end of the inning, measured on every 2026 regular season half-inning.

3. Each Plate Appearance

RE24 = RE after − RE before + runs scored. A hit that moves runners raises it, an out lowers it.

4. Each Player

Summed over the season: the runs a hitter added above an average hitter in the same situations. Then compared with his 2026 salary.

Model Methodology Data pipeline, worked examples, sanity check, and limitations ▼

RE24 answers a simple question: did this plate appearance make the team more or less likely to score? A single with the bases empty and a single with the bases loaded are both "one hit", but they do not change the inning in the same way. RE24 captures that difference, and it also counts outs, walks and solo home runs, which RBI-based measures miss.

Formula

RE24 (one PA) = RE(state after) − RE(state before) + runs scored on the play
RE24 (player) = sum over all his plate appearances

When a plate appearance ends the inning, the state after is worth 0. Because the matrix is built from league averages, a league-average hitter totals about 0 over a season: positive values are runs above average.

Worked Examples (2026 matrix)

Solo HR, 0 out0.500 − 0.500 + 1 = +1.00state unchanged, one run scores

Known Limitations

!Offense only

RE24 ignores defense and position. A strong defender with an average bat, like Ernie Clement, ranks low here and high on WAR.

!Baserunning inside a PA

Stolen bases and wild pitches during a plate appearance are credited to the batter. A full event-level file (Retrosheet) would separate them.

!Incomplete innings

Walk-offs end innings early. Summed over all players, RE24 totals −257 runs instead of 0, about 1% of runs scored, or 1 run per regular.

!Context included

RE24 rewards producing in big situations. It describes what happened, it does not isolate repeatable skill like wOBA or xwOBA.

Contract status bands: Pre-Arb under $1M, Arbitration from $1M to under $7M, Free Agent $7M and above. The full Python script that downloads the data and builds the matrix is reproducible from public data.

Key Findings

Salary still does not predict offensive run value, but RE24 is the first measure in this portfolio where free agent pay shows a faint signal.

Salary Barely Tracks Run Value

Across 263 batters, salary and RE24 per 600 PA correlate at r = 0.09 (p = 0.17). Among free agents only, the link rises to r = 0.19 (p = 0.08): close to significant, not quite.

The Cheapest Run Producers

Pete Crow-Armstrong (+48), Ben Rice (+41), Nick Kurtz (+38 in 436 PA) and James Wood (+37) all earn under $900K. Only Yordan Alvarez (+76, $26M) did better.

RE24 Rehabilitates On-Base Hitters

Steven Kwan ranks 121st on RE24 versus last on adjusted RBI, Mike Trout 76th versus 219th. Walks and solo home runs count here, not in RBI.

Expensive and Negative

Marcus Semien (−21, $26M), Jose Altuve (−21, $30M) and Trea Turner (−19, $27M) cost their teams runs. Lineup role does not explain it: their 2026 bats were below average.

So What?
The Best Offensive Measure Still Finds No Price for Production

RE24 is the most complete measure of offensive run production in this portfolio: it accounts for the situation, values every outcome including outs and walks, and counts solo home runs. Even with it, salary explains less than 1% of the variation (r = 0.09). Free agents add 5.4 runs per 600 PA above average, pre-arbitration players 3.4, at twenty times less money.

The free agent signal (r = 0.19, p = 0.08) is the most interesting result. It suggests that when teams set salaries freely, they do pay a little for offense, but much less than salary differences would imply. Most of what a large contract buys is something else: defense, durability, or past seasons. The Moneyball and Contract Value analyses look at those.

For a front office, the practical use is a screening tool: hitters like Nick Kurtz, Ben Rice or Gabriel Moreno produce run value at the level of $20M+ veterans. The natural next step is to compare RE24 with a context-neutral measure such as wOBA: hitters with a high RE24 but an average wOBA were productive in big moments, which is less likely to repeat. See also the RBI per PA vs. Salary analysis, which this page extends.

Glossary

Run Value (RE24) vs. Salary
Pos
Primary position: the one played most often that season, per Baseball Reference (C, 1B, 2B, 3B, SS, LF, CF, RF, DH). Shohei Ohtani is listed DH/SP.
RE24
Run Expectancy based on the 24 base-out states. The change in expected runs caused by a plate appearance, plus any runs that scored on the play. Summed over a season, it measures how many runs a hitter added compared with an average hitter in the same situations.
Base-Out State
One of the 24 game situations defined by which bases are occupied (8 combinations, from bases empty to bases loaded) and how many outs there are (0, 1 or 2).
Run Expectancy
The average number of runs scored from a given base-out state until the end of the half-inning, computed from every 2026 regular season half-inning.
Run Expectancy Matrix
The table of the 24 run expectancy values. Read it as: with this many outs and these runners on base, an average team scores this many more runs in the inning.
RE24 per 600 PA
RE24 divided by plate appearances, multiplied by 600 (roughly a full season). Compares part-time and full-time hitters on the same scale.
Play-by-Play Data
A record of every plate appearance of the season, with the game situation before and after it. Here taken from Baseball Savant (Statcast), pitch by pitch.
Statcast
MLB tracking system in all 30 ballparks. Its public database on Baseball Savant records every pitch, including outs, runners on base and the score.
RBI
Runs Batted In. Runs that score as a direct result of a batter’s plate appearance.
Adjusted RBI/PA
RBI per plate appearance divided by a team context index, from the RBI per PA vs. Salary analysis.
BRS%
Share of runners on base that a batter drove in, from the RBI per PA vs. Salary analysis.
Pearson r
Correlation coefficient, from -1 to +1. Values near 0 mean no linear relationship.
p-value
Probability of seeing a relationship this strong by chance alone if there were no real link. Below 0.05 is the usual threshold for significance.
Pre-Arb
Players in their first years of MLB service, earning near the league minimum. Proxied here by a 2026 salary under $1M.
Arbitration
Players with roughly 3 to 6 years of service whose salaries go through a formal process. Proxied here by $1M to under $7M.
Free Agent
Players on market-rate contracts. Proxied here by a salary of $7M or more.
